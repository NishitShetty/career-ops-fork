## C) Level and Strategy

**Level detected:** Senior/Staff IC. **Candidate's natural level:** Senior Technical/Delivery Lead — aligns well.

**Where this fits the plan.** Phase 1 wants a GTA role at an employer with a US entity, ideally manager-track. This delivers **three of four**: GTA location ✓, US entity for a later transfer ✓, strong comp ✓ — but it is **IC, so it accrues no managerial-capacity record**.

That trade is more defensible than it first looks:

- **External hire into first-time people management is genuinely hard.** Employers de-risk by hiring proven managers; a *Senior* EM posting typically expects 2+ years managing, sometimes managing managers. The candidate has no formal direct reports.
- **Internal IC→manager conversion is materially easier than external hire into management.** The realistic sequence is: land a strong IC seat in the GTA at a large employer, then convert internally — or move to a manager title from a much stronger base.
- Intermediate rungs that are easier to reach than Senior EM: **Tech Lead Manager**, **first-level EM** (not Senior EM), and **TPM→EM**.

**Caveat specific to Tesla:** Tesla runs comparatively flat with fewer management layers than Google/Amazon/Lyft, so internal conversion to people management may be *harder* here than at a more conventionally layered employer. Ask directly in interviews what the management track looks like.

**"Sell senior without lying" plan:** Lead with the NMS parallel in one sentence — *"I've spent eleven years building the monitoring, aggregation and remote-control layer for fleets of distributed devices; the endpoint was a satellite terminal instead of a Powerwall."* Follow with the low-level OS evidence (bootloader audit, leak profiling), which is unusually strong for a cloud-services candidate and directly answers a stated requirement most applicants will fail.

**"If they downlevel me" plan:** The band is wide (CAD 184.8-330K), so leveling matters more than title here. If offered near the bottom, negotiate on the NMS-parallel depth and the low-level OS match rather than on years.
