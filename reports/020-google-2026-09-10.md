## C) Level and Strategy

**The level is the main thing to negotiate.** The req is posted at **Mid**, which maps to **L4 (TPM II)**. He has 11+ years.

Two readings, both fair:

- **L4 is a realistic landing spot for a first Google TPM role.** His title is *Product Delivery Lead & Senior Software Engineer*, not TPM. Google levels on demonstrated scope against its own ladder, and without a TPM title the loop will anchor conservatively.
- **11 years against a 5-year minimum is a down-level**, and the cost is material — see Block D.

**Strategy:** apply, and raise levelling in the recruiter screen rather than after an offer. Ask directly whether the req is fixed at L4 or whether the loop can level to L5 given the experience. If it is fixed, L4→L5 internal promotion at Google is a normal, well-worn path and the clearance makes him harder to replace once inside.

**How to position:** lead with **program delivery under contractual obligation**, not with SatCom. The reframe is available without inventing anything — Inmarsat Site Acceptance Testing in Michigan and Perth, Regulated Verification Testing at 100% stability, and the aviation traffic-drop debug resolved before the aircraft landed are all delivery against external commitments with visible consequences. That is the same muscle the MSA/SoW language describes, exercised commercially instead of in government.

**Do not lead with the architecture work** — it is the strongest differentiator, but it lands better as the *second* thing, after the delivery framing has established him as a TPM rather than an engineer applying sideways.
